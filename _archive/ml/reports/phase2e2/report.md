# Phase 2E.2: Observation sufficiency and counterfactual diagnosis

**This is an analysis only.** No model was trained, retrained, fine-tuned or modified. None of these changed:
- the observation, action space, act-v3, completion shield, planner, simulator, rule bots, player data, auction order, scoring, purse distribution, production runtime, the 20 ms guard, or any frozen hash.

Every number below comes from **read-only replays** of recorded Phase 2E.0 episodes. Each replayed episode reproduced its recorded learner-action, auction and summary digests. The replay captured the exact 80-feature vector the frozen policy received at each decision.

Language is associational unless stated otherwise. **Nothing here prescribes an observation change**, and no algorithm is ranked.

## Summary

**Decision gate: RESULT B, PARTIALLY SUFFICIENT.** Most of the strategically important information is present. Several signals are missing, pooled into aggregates, or clipped until they carry almost nothing. Before Stage-B training, the observation spec needs a targeted discussion. The data support a discussion; they do not show that the observation is unusable.

What is present:

- **Own state, supply and rival purses.** Own purse, squad, overseas and requirement state, the remaining supply (including returning players) and rival purse summaries are all encoded directly.
- **Room-level opponent pressure.** It is encoded indirectly but strongly after the first ~5–10% of the main round.
  - Rival purse statistics separate rooms with four high-pressure copies (C4 with D3QN/QR-DQN) from Stage-A rooms almost perfectly (|2·AUC−1| ≥ 0.9).
  - The frozen caps still move with the policy's **own purse**, not with these signals:
    - same-lot ΔCap vs Δ own purse: ρ 0.08–0.38;
    - ΔCap vs Δ recent price ratio: ρ −0.15 to +0.16.
  - 58.5% of learner observations in those rooms have an opponent-driven feature outside the Stage-A range.
  - So for adaptation, **the information exists but the frozen policies do not use it**. This is consistent with values the policies never saw in Stage A. It is not proven to be the cause.
- **Keeper failures (M1) were avoidable with observable information.**
  - All 299 of the M1 passes were on affordable keepers that filled a visible own need.
  - 203 of those keepers went unsold.
- **Rival keeper holdings are largely inferable.** About 91% of the variance in the maximum keepers held by one rival lies between cells of the observed keeper features.

What is missing or coarsened:

1. **Opponent bidding propensity before sales reveal it.**
   - The first Stage-B observation is bit-identical to Stage A in every episode.
   - Signals stay faint until about lot 16. By then 24% of the stars have been auctioned.
2. **How many rivals are aggressive.**
   - The rival block holds only symmetric aggregates.
   - In 5% of the C1-vs-C4 pairs with at least 2 more RL bidders, all 17 rival and window features match within 0.02.
3. **Keeper demand from rivals who do not need one.**
   - The observation, the planner and the shield all count only rivals that *need* a keeper.
   - At the M1 forced bids that were lost, 0.34 rivals needed a keeper on average, against 5.0 non-needing rivals able to buy one.
4. **Five features are pinned at the clip value 5 in ≥ 97% of decisions:**
   - `riv_capacity_1–3`: 99.2%, 98.4% and 97.4%;
   - `mkt_scarcity_bowling`: 99.7%;
   - `mkt_scarcity_indians`: 99.8%.

**Scorecard** (Analysis K):
- 2 × YES: opponent behaviour at room level; own squad/overseas slot filling.
- 4 × PARTIAL: early star contests; multiple aggressive opponents; keeper failures; requirement timing and shield dependence.
- 1 × UNKNOWN: seed-specific weaknesses.

**Mechanism categories** (Analysis B, across the 14 mechanisms):
- A (direct): 4.
- B (indirect): 3.
- C (partial): 7.
- D (not observable): 0.

Three structural facts matter for everything that follows:

1. **The only explicit history** is a 20-lot window pooled over all buyers: `mkt_recent_price_ratio`, `mkt_recent_sold_share` and `riv_recent_spend`.
   - It carries no buyer identity and no role breakdown.

## 2. Data and method

- **Replayed episodes.** 40 of the 500 validation entries were replayed under all 390 Phase 2E.0 cells, giving 15,600 episodes (`raw/replay-entries.json`).
  - 19 entries contain a Phase 2E.0 finding.
  - 13 were drawn at random, using RandomState(20252).
  - 8 more (4 low, 4 normal) were added to balance the strata, using RandomState(20253).
  - Strata: high 20, normal 10, low 10.
  - Every episode was digest-verified, with 0 mismatches (`raw/replay.log`).
- **Rows captured: 2,659,482.**
  - 2,186,930 are learner decisions: the exact `ep.pending.obs`.
  - 472,552 are RL-opponent decisions, covering every keeper-lot decision plus every decision of the 20 finding seats.
  - Opponent observations were rebuilt with the frozen `buildRlObservation`. For argmax exports the rebuilt vector had to reproduce the runtime's action, and every one did.
- **Hidden state stored beside each observation:**
  - the caps of the other nine seats on that lot (read by a pass-through wrapper on the simulator instance);
  - the price needed to win (their maximum cap / fair value, capped at 5);
  - per-rival keeper holdings and needs;
  - the 20-lot window split by buyer;
  - the lot outcome.
- **Methods: descriptive only.** Nothing was trained or fitted, and no classifier or network was used. The statistics are:
  - paired differences;
  - Spearman rank correlations *within progress bins*, which removes the shared auction clock;
  - Mann–Whitney separation |2·AUC−1|;
  - L∞ distances;
  - between-cell variance shares.
- **Thresholds** are stated in every JSON file:
  - "aliased" means L∞ ≤ 0.02 over all 80 features, with 0.05 as a looser level;
  - "material" means the price needed to win differs by ≥ 0.5 × fair value;
  - "similar own state" means purse share within ±0.05, squad ±1, overseas ±1, and the same keeper need.

## 4. Analysis B: Observability categories

- **A: direct.**
  - Rival purse pressure.
  - Price inflation (recent).
  - Requirement timing.
  - Purse depletion.
- **B: indirect.**
  - Opponent aggressiveness at room level. No single feature carries it, but rival-purse statistics and the window together separate rooms almost perfectly after the early lots.
  - Rival keeper demand. It can be recovered from scarcity × supply × own need.
  - Re-auction pressure.
- **C: partial.**
  - Early star competition. The contest-deciding component, the opponents' propensity, is invisible early.
  - Multiple aggressive opponents.
  - Rival role demand. Bowling and Indians demand is saturated or pooled.
  - Rival keeper stockpiling. Holdings are inferable; willingness to buy is not.
  - Overseas competition.
  - Squad-slot competition.
  - Final-path risk.
- **D: not observable.** No mechanism falls wholly in D. Two *components* are D:
  - opponent identity or propensity before any sale;
  - the willingness of non-needing rivals to buy a keeper.

  Each sits inside a C mechanism, next to information that is observable.

### Answering the key question

> At the exact point where the learner passed on the keeper, could the current observation distinguish this dangerous state from a genuinely safe keeper state?

**Through its keeper signals, no.**

1. **The keeper signals looked like a safe state.**
   - At the dangerous decisions, the keeper-specific observed features sit in the middle of the matched safe distribution. Median percentiles:
     - `self_need_keeper`: 0.50;
     - `self_status_keeper`: 0.48;
     - `riv_fills_share`: 0.46;
     - `mkt_scarcity_keeper`: 0.38;
     - `mkt_supply_Wi`: 0.27.
   - The shield uses the same quantities and rated **80% of M1 keeper decisions SAFE**.
   - The planner status read NEED, not CRITICAL, in 60% of them.
2. **The dangerous states are not identical to safe ones, but the differences lie elsewhere.**
   - The nearest safe decision is within 0.05 in the keeper sub-space for only 12% of dangerous decisions (median 0.17).
   - The largest gaps are in `mkt_recent_price_ratio`, `mkt_scarcity_keeper`, `riv_free_slots_share`, `riv_able_share` and `self_max_safe_fv`, and in own-state features (purse percentile 0.30, slots 0.24).
   - So these states are **distinguishable, but not by a signal that reads as "keeper danger"**.
3. **Even the hidden current rival state was not extreme at the pass decisions.**
   - Median percentiles: max keepers held by one rival 0.60; rivals holding ≥ 2 keepers 0.70; non-needing rivals able to buy 0.56.
   - These were above the 90th percentile in only 13%, 22% and 7% of decisions.
   - The danger was mostly **in the future**: non-needing rivals kept buying the remaining keepers after the passes.
   - 298 of the 299 M1 passes came after 50% progress: 204 late in the main round, 94 in the re-auction.

### M1 and M2 differ in observability

- **M1 (14 cases).**
  - Every one of the 299 passes was on a keeper that was **affordable** (maxSafeBid ≥ base) and **filled the visible own need** (`self_need_keeper` = 1, `self_fills_keeper` = 1). 203 of them went unsold.
  - The information needed to avoid the failure, "I need a keeper and can buy this one now", was **observable**.
  - The information needed to judge the **risk of deferring**, whether non-needing rivals will absorb the remaining keepers, was **not**.
  - At the 35 forced bids that were lost, 0.34 rivals needed a keeper on average, against 5.0 non-needing rivals able to buy one.
- **M2 (6 cases).**
  - Own purse and overseas exhaustion in the first quarter is **directly visible**: `self_purse`, `self_overseas_slots_left`, `self_max_safe_*`, `self_reserve_if_passed`.
  - 40 forced bids were lost to rivals with large purses (`riv_purse_max`, visible).
  - At those bids, 1.0 rival needed a keeper on average, against 5.65 non-needing able rivals.

- In the M1 cases, the lost forced bids went mostly to such rivals.
- Evidence suggests this demand from non-needing rivals may be contributing to the keeper failures. It should be investigated during Stage-B design.

## 9. Analysis G: Observational aliasing

- **Criterion.** Two captured decisions on the same entry, phase and lot, from different episodes or seats, are aliased when all 80 features lie within L∞ ≤ 0.02. 345,772,627 pairs were examined (plot 07).

| Situation difference (material) | Pairs | Aliased (≤ 0.02) | Rate | Aliased with a different lot outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| price needed differs by ≥ 0.5 × fair | 71,647,766 | 89,758 | 0.13% | 18,571 |
| Stage-A room vs C4 D3QN/QR-DQN room | 4,033,693 | 42,858 | 1.06% | 3,485 |
| one copy (C1) vs four copies (C4) | 9,742,255 | 100,251 | 1.03% | 7,183 |
| RL rivals bidding differ by ≥ 2 | 147,869,096 | 1,700,658 | 1.15% | 128,187 |
| keeper competition (stockpile) differs | 1,584,467 | 0 | 0.00% | 0 |
| safe vs dangerous keeper seat | 34,994 | 276 | 0.79% | 5 |
| mean rival purse differs ≥ 0.10 (control, encoded) | 69,820,347 | 0 | 0.00% | 0 |

Aliasing in the strict sense of near-identical vectors is **rare** (549 lots are affected for price pressure). In the same-lot pairs of Analysis F it occurs only before 10% progress.
- Materially different situations usually produce **different** observations.
- The limitation is resolution and aggregation, not identical vectors:
  - the differences are often not in a feature that measures the relevant quantity (Analyses C, E, J);
  - the capacity features are saturated.
- Aliasing therefore does **not** show that the relevant information is absent. That question is answered structurally, in Analyses B–D.

The single window is **pooled** across buyers and roles, and **short** (20 lots, about 6% of the main round).
- Cumulative behaviour survives in rival purses, and that is where the strong room-level separation comes from.
- *Who* did what, and *which roles* were bought, are not recoverable.
- The first lots carry no history at all:
  - the obs-v2 defaults fill the window (price ratio 1, sold share 1);
  - 1.4% of stars are auctioned at lot 1, 7.3% by lot 5 and 23.9% by lot 16.

## 14. Critical decision gate: RESULT B, PARTIALLY SUFFICIENT

The details are in `decision-gate.json`.

> **Implication:** Stage-B design requires a targeted observation-spec discussion before training.

- **Why not A (sufficient):**
  - opponent propensity is invisible at the start of the auction;
  - the number of aggressive rivals and non-needing keeper demand are not encoded;
  - five features are clipped in ≥ 97% of decisions.
- **Why not C (insufficient):**
  - No major mechanism depends *entirely* on absent information.
  - Room-level aggressiveness is encoded from ~5–10% of the main round, and the frozen policies do not respond to it.
  - Every M1 keeper failure passed on an affordable keeper that filled a visible need.
- **Why not D (unknown):** the replays settle, feature by feature, what is present, absent, aggregated or saturated.
  - The one open question is whether a policy *would* use the present signals if trained on Stage-B data.
  - That is a learning question, and outside this phase.

Evidence suggests these may be contributing to Stage-B XI loss:
- the missing early-auction propensity signal;
- aggregated rival features;
- saturated capacity and scarcity features;
- demand from non-needing keeper buyers that is not encoded.

They should be investigated during Stage-B design. This report does not specify any feature to add or change.

## 15. Limitations

- **Coverage.**
  - The replays cover 40 of the 500 validation entries: all 19 entries with a finding, plus 21 random ones. The high stratum is over-represented (20 of 40).
  - Condition-level statements use all 390 cells on those entries.
- **Small sample for keeper conclusions.** They rest on 20 findings: 467 keeper decisions, 35 lost M1 forced bids and 40 lost M2 forced bids.
- **Price needed is an approximation.** It uses the other seats' caps on the realised trajectory. Once a learner's choices differ, the rooms differ, which is why paired analyses also require a similar own state.
- **Thresholds are conventions:** 0.02 / 0.05 for aliasing, 0.5 × fair for "material", 0.5 / 0.2 for correlation strength. The JSON files carry the raw values.
- **Stage-A range is a proxy.** The Stage-A range is measured on Stage-A validation rooms, not on the training manifest.
- **What the data cannot show:** whether any policy could learn to use a signal, which would need training. Causation is not established. The findings are associations on frozen behaviour.
